Course Description

Bi 122. Genetics. 9 units (3-0-6); first term. Prerequisites: Bi 8 or Bi 9, or instructor's permission. Lecture and discussion course covering basic principles of genetics. Not open to freshmen. Instructors: Hay, Staff.

Welcome to Bi 122! This course covers a broad range of topics in genetics, from the classic experiments of Mendel to the modern sciences of gene regulation and genomics.

Lectures are usually supplemented with handouts summarizing the material covered. Because a good deal of what is presented in class does not appear in the book, it is worth attending at least a few of the lectures.

Recommended text
An Introduction to Genetic Analysis
A. J. F. Griffiths et al. · 12th edition
W. H. Freeman and Company
ISBN 978-1-319-28646-0

Referred to as “G” in the schedule below.

Chapters 7–9 should be reviewed — skimmed for terms and general sense — if you have not taken Bi 8. If you are unsure of your mastery of the Bi 8/9 material, or came from another department or school without an equivalent course, read those chapters. For some parts of the course the book does not provide adequate information, or presents it with a slant different from what will be covered in class.

Problem sets

Problem sets are handed out on Thursday after class and due back by the following Thursday at 11:59 pm — except the last one, which will be handed out on a date to be determined.

Try to get them in on time. Sets less than 24 hours late lose 10% of the grade; up to 48 hours late, 20%. In general, problem sets should not be handed in later than that.

Sets are graded from 0 to 100%. In calculating the final grade the lowest score is discarded; the remaining seven count equally and together make up 60% of the final grade.

If something comes up Illness reported with a valid notice from the Health Center — and other life-impacting events — are accepted as reason for late work without penalty. The important thing is to tell the TAs and Professor Hay in a timely manner so we can work with you.
Letter grades
A 91–100% B 81–90% C 71–80% D 65–70% F < 65%

Sometimes a test turns out to be much harder than we thought it would be. If that happens we will change the conversion of points to grades so that a score lower than 91% might be an A, and so on. Any change of this sort will be announced.

We will not change the conversion in the opposite direction — under any circumstances, a 91% is an A.

Exam format The midterm and final are limited-time, in-person, open-book exams.

Collaboration Policy

Problem sets. You must work completely independently on each problem set for three hours. After that you are free to discuss the problems. You may use visual aids during these discussions — the digital equivalent of chalkboards, marker boards, or scratch paper.

You are not permitted, in these discussions, to use another student's answer to any problem when it is in its final form, ready to submit. You must write up your solutions independently of others, and not use any recorded notes of discussion sessions. There is to be no division of effort.

Exams. No collaboration, either verbal or written. Personal calculators are permitted. Each exam counts for one-fifth of the final grade.
